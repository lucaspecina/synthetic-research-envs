# Seed: What Should We Measure Next? Groundwater Contamination

## Type
Value of information / evidence design prioritization (scenario #23)

## Domain
Environmental science / hydrogeology / study design

## Study design
A regional authority suspects groundwater contamination from agricultural
runoff is causing health issues in rural communities. They have a limited
budget for additional measurements. Current data includes well water samples,
land use records, and health registry data for 300 communities. Several
variables are poorly measured or unmeasured. The question is NOT "what causes
the contamination" but "what additional measurement would most improve our
ability to answer the causal question?"

## Variables
- Nitrate level in wells (continuous, measured but noisy)
- Pesticide concentration (continuous, only 40% of wells sampled)
- Proximity to agricultural land (continuous)
- Soil permeability (continuous)
- Depth to water table (continuous, only 60% known)
- Gastrointestinal illness rate (continuous)
- Cancer incidence (continuous, long lag)
- Population density (continuous)
- Well age and construction type (categorical)
- Rainfall intensity (continuous)
- Upstream livestock density (continuous, poorly measured)
- Fertilizer application rate (poorly measured, estimated from sales data)

## Key findings to inspire
- Current data suggests nitrate-health association but unidentifiable causally
- Reason: fertilizer_rate is a key confounder but unmeasured (only proxy exists)
- Measuring depth_to_water_table (cheap) would enable better adjustment
- Measuring pesticide_concentration in remaining wells (moderate cost) would
  reveal whether nitrate is a proxy for a broader contamination profile
- Measuring fertilizer_rate directly (expensive) would be most informative
  but cost-prohibitive
- The VALUE of each measurement depends on how much it reduces confounding
  bias, not just on the measurement itself

## Why this is interesting for SREG
The claims should be about WHAT TO MEASURE, not about causal effects. "Depth
to water table is the highest-value measurement because it enables adjustment
for a key confounder at low cost" is a study design recommendation. "Current
nitrate data is insufficient for causal claims because fertilizer rate is
unmeasured" is about evidence sufficiency. These don't map to "X causes Y."
