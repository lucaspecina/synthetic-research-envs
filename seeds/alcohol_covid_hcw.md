# Paper Seed: Alcohol Consumption and COVID-19 Stress Among Healthcare Workers

## Source
Beiter KJ et al. (2022) "Alcohol Consumption and COVID-19-Related Stress Among Health Care Workers"
Public Health Reports, 137(2):326-335. Louisiana State University Health Sciences Center.

## Domain
Occupational health / mental health / substance use epidemiology

## Study design
Mixed-methods cross-sectional survey. N=102 healthcare workers at academic hospital in New Orleans
during first COVID-19 wave (June 2020). ~18.5% response rate from 550 distributed surveys.

## Variables measured

### Outcome
- Alcohol consumption: AUDIT-C score (0-12), measured pre-COVID and during COVID
- Change in AUDIT-C score (primary outcome in regression)

### Exposures
- Work-related stress (MERS-CoV Staff Questionnaire stress subscale)
- Emotional reactions (MERS-CoV emotions subscale)
- Coping strategies (Brief COPE): social support, avoidant, self-sufficient

### Mental health covariates
- Depressive symptoms: PHQ-9 (0-27, >=10 clinically significant)
- PTSD symptoms: PC-PTSD (0-5, >=3 clinically significant)

### Demographic covariates
- Sex, race (78.4% White), age (5 categories)
- Years of experience (mean 15.8, SD 13.0)
- Marital status (79.4% married/partner)
- Living with children (55.9%)
- Professional role (66.7% physicians, 14.7% nurses)
- Department (emergency, ICU, surgery, internal medicine, psychiatry)

## Key findings
- Stress correlated with increased alcohol: r=0.30 (p=.006)
- Depression strongly associated: PHQ-9 r=0.47 (p<.001)
- Avoidant coping: r=0.45 (p<.001) with alcohol change
- Single status associated with greater increase (+1.3 vs +0.1 for married)
- Nurses showed greater increase than physicians (+1.1 vs +0.2)
- Race associated: White +2.4 points vs Black (p=.009)
- Social isolation largest qualitative theme (47.1%)

## Research questions
- How does work stress affect alcohol consumption during a pandemic?
- What coping mechanisms mediate the stress-alcohol relationship?
- Does marital/social status modify the effect?
- What demographic factors predict increased consumption?
- Is avoidant coping a key mechanism, or just a correlate?

## Causal complexity
- Confounders: race, age, professional role all affect both stress exposure and alcohol
- Mediators: avoidant coping may mediate stress -> alcohol pathway
- Effect modifiers: marital status modifies the stress-alcohol relationship
- Possible latent: baseline alcohol tolerance/dependence (not measured directly)
- Selection bias: 18.5% response rate — who responded?
- Social desirability bias: qualitative reports contradict quantitative scores
