# Paper Seed: Coral Reef Bleaching and Recovery

## Source
Based on: Hughes TP et al. (2017) "Global warming and recurrent mass bleaching of corals"
Nature 543:373-377. + Hughes TP et al. (2018) "Spatial and temporal patterns of mass
bleaching of corals in the Anthropocene" Science 359:80-83.

## Domain
Marine ecology / coral reef science / climate change impacts

## Study design
Large-scale observational study. Aerial and underwater surveys of >1000 reefs along
2300km of the Great Barrier Reef. Longitudinal comparison across bleaching events
(1998, 2002, 2016, 2017). Quantified bleaching severity, mortality, recovery rates.

## Variables

### Outcome
- Coral bleaching severity (none, mild, moderate, severe)
- Coral mortality (% colony death within 8 months)
- Reef recovery (partial, full, none after 12 months)

### Exposures
- Sea surface temperature anomaly (degree heating weeks, DHW)
- Cumulative thermal stress (duration x intensity above bleaching threshold)
- Prior bleaching history (number of previous events)

### Ecological covariates
- Coral community composition (branching vs massive vs encrusting)
- Coral cover (% of reef surface)
- Reef depth (shallow, mid, deep)
- Water quality (turbidity, nutrient levels)
- Herbivore fish biomass (grazing pressure)
- Algal cover (competitor abundance)
- Crown-of-thorns starfish presence

### Geographic/environmental
- Latitude (proxy for baseline temperature regime)
- Distance from coast (water quality gradient)
- Reef type (fringing, barrier, platform)
- Local human impact (fishing pressure, runoff)
- Marine protected area status (yes/no)

## Key findings
- Bleaching severity primarily driven by thermal stress (DHW), not local factors
- Prior bleaching reduced coral's ability to recover (cumulative damage)
- Branching corals most vulnerable, massive corals most resistant
- Deep reefs partially buffered from thermal stress
- Marine protected areas did NOT reduce bleaching (climate overwhelms local management)
- Recovery time: 10-15 years minimum for full recovery
- Third global bleaching event (2016-17) unprecedented in severity

## Research questions
- What is the probability of severe bleaching given observed thermal stress and reef characteristics?
- If thermal stress could be reduced (e.g., cloud brightening), how would bleaching severity change?
- Does prior bleaching history causally reduce recovery capacity, or is it confounded by reef quality?
- Which reef characteristic (depth, composition, water quality) best predicts resilience?
- Should management prioritize water quality improvement or is climate the overwhelming driver?

## Causal complexity
- Confounders: latitude affects both temperature regime and coral community composition
- Mediators: thermal stress -> bleaching -> algal overgrowth -> reduced recovery
- Effect modifiers: coral composition modifies the temperature-bleaching relationship
- Possible latent: genetic thermal tolerance (not directly measurable)
- Temporal: cumulative stress across events matters, not just acute stress
- Scale: reef-level vs colony-level responses differ
