# Paper Seed: Socioeconomic Factors and Student Academic Performance

## Source
Based on: Sirin SR (2005) "Socioeconomic Status and Academic Achievement: A Meta-Analytic
Review of Research" Review of Educational Research 75(3):417-453. + Coleman Report (1966)
"Equality of Educational Opportunity" + PISA 2022 results (OECD).

## Domain
Educational research / sociology of education / policy evaluation

## Study design
Large-scale observational analysis combining school-level and student-level data.
Standardized test scores linked with demographic, family, school, and neighborhood
characteristics. Cross-sectional with some longitudinal cohort tracking.
Typical samples: 5000-50000 students across hundreds of schools.

## Variables

### Outcome
- Academic achievement (standardized test score in math/reading)
- Grade point average
- High school completion (yes/no)

### Family-level exposures
- Family income (continuous or quintiles)
- Parental education level (none, primary, secondary, tertiary)
- Parental occupation (manual, service, professional)
- Family structure (two-parent, single-parent, other)
- Home educational resources (books, internet, study space)
- Parental involvement (meetings, homework help, expectations)

### Student-level characteristics
- Prior academic performance (baseline score)
- Student motivation/engagement
- Study time (hours per week)
- Absenteeism (days missed per year)
- Peer group quality
- Special education needs (yes/no)
- Language at home (same as instruction or different)

### School-level variables
- School funding per pupil
- Teacher quality (certification, experience, degrees)
- Class size
- School type (public, private, charter)
- School disciplinary climate
- Availability of extracurricular programs
- School socioeconomic composition (% low-income students)

### Neighborhood/contextual
- Neighborhood poverty rate
- Urban/suburban/rural location
- Crime rate
- Access to libraries and cultural institutions

## Key findings
- SES-achievement correlation: r=0.30 (meta-analytic mean), stronger at school level (r=0.73)
- Family income explains ~20% of variance in test scores
- Parental education is stronger predictor than income alone
- School composition (% low-income peers) has independent effect beyond individual SES
- Teacher quality matters but is confounded with school funding
- Prior achievement is strongest predictor of current achievement (r=0.70)
- Private school advantage largely disappears after controlling for SES

## Research questions
- What is the causal effect of family income on academic achievement, beyond confounders?
- Does school composition have an independent causal effect (peer effects)?
- Is the private school advantage real or just selection bias?
- What is the most effective intervention point: family income, teacher quality, or class size?
- Does parental education mediate or confound the income-achievement relationship?
- Are there latent factors (e.g., cognitive ability, family stability) that explain both SES and achievement?

## Causal complexity
- Confounders: parental education confounds income-achievement (causes both)
- Mediators: parental involvement may mediate SES -> achievement
- Selection bias: private school enrollment is self-selected by SES
- Latent variables: innate cognitive ability, family cultural capital
- Multilevel: student nested in schools nested in neighborhoods
- Reverse causality: achievement affects motivation affects achievement
- Effect heterogeneity: SES effect differs by race, gender, school type
