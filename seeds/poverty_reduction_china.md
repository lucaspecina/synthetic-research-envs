# Paper Seed: Poverty Reduction Program Effects on Behavior

## Source
"A multifaceted poverty reduction program has economic and behavioral consequences"
PMC10013832, Hubei Province China, fuzzy regression discontinuity design.

## Domain
Development economics / behavioral economics / poverty policy evaluation

## Study design
Fuzzy regression discontinuity. 2039 rural households, 60% treated. Program assigned
based on income threshold — households just below more likely to receive. Quasi-random
variation near the cutoff. Lab-in-the-field experiments for behavioral outcomes.

## Variables
- Per capita household income (business, wage, rental, transfers)
- Income growth 2013-2018
- Consistency with rational decision-making (Afriat CCEI)
- Distributional preferences: selfishness, equality preference, efficiency preference
- Beliefs about fairness and social mobility
- Treatment status (program recipient vs not)
- Base-year income (determines assignment)
- County, village, household size

## Key findings
- Program increased income 40-50% over 5 years
- Increased efficiency preference by 27.7 percentage points
- Reduced selfishness by 0.77 standard deviations
- No change in equality preference
- Behavioral effects concentrated in villages with higher treatment concentration

## Research questions
- Does poverty reduction change social preferences (not just income)?
- Which preferences change and which don't?
- Does the behavioral effect depend on community-level treatment intensity?
- Is the income effect causal or selection?

## Causal complexity
- RD design: quasi-random near threshold, but manipulation possible
- Mediator: income → behavioral change (or direct program effect?)
- Confounder: village-level characteristics affect both treatment and outcomes
- Heterogeneity: effect varies by treatment concentration in village
- Latent: underlying risk preferences, social norms
